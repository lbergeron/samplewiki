# How to Choose the Right Wetsuit

A wetsuit should keep you warm without restricting paddling or making movement uncomfortable. Water temperature, wind, session length, and personal tolerance all matter.

## Choose the thickness

Wetsuit thickness is usually listed in millimeters, such as 3/2 or 4/3. The first number describes the thicker torso panel and the second describes thinner limb panels.

As a general guide:

- **Warm water:** Rash guard, springsuit, or thin shorty may be enough.
- **Cool water:** A 2/2 or 3/2 full suit is common.
- **Cold water:** A 4/3 or thicker suit, often with boots, gloves, and a hood.

Local recommendations are more reliable than generic temperature ranges because wind, currents, and body size affect comfort.

## Prioritize fit

A wetsuit should sit close to the skin with no large folds or gaps. It should allow full shoulder rotation, a deep knee bend, and comfortable breathing. A little resistance when putting it on is normal; pain, numbness, or restricted breathing is not.

Check that:

- The neck seals without choking you.
- The back or chest closure lies flat.
- Sleeves and legs reach the wrists and ankles.
- There is no large pocket of air at the lower back.
- You can paddle and raise your arms normally.

Do not size by height and weight alone. Try the suit on and compare the manufacturer's size chart.

## Choose useful features

Chest zips can provide flexibility, while back zips may be easier to put on. Sealed or liquid-taped seams reduce water entry in colder conditions. Reinforced knees improve durability and comfort when popping up.

## Put it on carefully

Use the pads of your fingers, not fingernails, to pull the neoprene. Work the suit gradually from the legs upward and smooth folds before closing the zip. Avoid forcing a stuck zipper.

## Care and replacement

Rinse with fresh water after every session, dry inside out in shade, and store on a wide hanger or folded without sharp creases. Replace a suit when thinning, tears, stretched seals, or loss of flexibility make it cold or unsafe.

## Fitting checklist

- [ ] I can breathe and rotate my shoulders freely.
- [ ] The suit has no major gaps or folds.
- [ ] The thickness matches my local water and wind.
- [ ] I can put it on without pulling the seams.
- [ ] I know how to rinse and dry it properly.
