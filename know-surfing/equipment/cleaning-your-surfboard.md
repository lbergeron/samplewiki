# How to Clean Your Surfboard Properly

Regular cleaning improves grip, reveals damage, and helps prevent salt and sand from wearing equipment. Use gentle methods and keep the board away from unnecessary heat.

## What you need

- A cool, shaded work area.
- A plastic wax comb or old card.
- A soft cloth or sponge.
- Fresh water.
- A mild, board-safe cleaner if needed.
- A towel and fresh wax for the water temperature.

Avoid sharp metal tools, harsh solvents, and abrasive cleaners.

## Remove old wax

1. Let the board cool in the shade so the wax is firm but workable.
2. Use the comb’s flat edge to lift wax in small sections.
3. Scrape away from fittings and avoid pressing hard on soft spots.
4. Brush or wipe away remaining crumbs.
5. Use a board-safe cleaner sparingly for stubborn residue.

Do not leave a board in direct sun to melt wax. Excessive heat can cause delamination, bubbles, or other damage.

## Rinse and inspect

Rinse the board with fresh water after saltwater sessions. Wipe the deck, bottom, rails, fin boxes, leash plug, and traction pad. Dry it before putting it in a bag.

Check for:

- Cracks, open dings, or exposed foam.
- Soft spots or raised bubbles.
- Loose fins or a moving leash plug.
- Lifted traction-pad edges.
- Sand trapped around fittings.

Keep a board with exposed foam out of the water until repaired.

## Reapply wax

When the surface is clean and dry, apply a base coat if appropriate, followed by wax suited to the water temperature. Build small, even bumps where your feet, chest, and hands need grip.

Replace wax when it becomes smooth, dirty, or difficult to comb.

## Storage checklist

- [ ] The board is rinsed and completely dry.
- [ ] Fins are protected or removed for transport.
- [ ] The leash is rinsed and untangled.
- [ ] The board is stored in shade, away from heat.
- [ ] Damage is repaired before the next session.
