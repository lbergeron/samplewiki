# Surfing Equipment Maintenance Guide

Small maintenance tasks prevent avoidable failures and help equipment last longer. Rinse salt and sand away, dry everything fully, and repair damage before returning to the water.

## Before every session

- Check the board for cracks, soft spots, sharp edges, and loose fittings.
- Inspect the leash cord, swivels, cuff, railsaver, and plug.
- Confirm fins and screws are secure without overtightening.
- Check wax or traction for reliable grip.
- Make sure the wetsuit has no tears or failed seals.

Do not surf a board with exposed foam or a leash that is frayed or damaged.

## After every session

1. Rinse the board, fins, leash, and wetsuit with fresh water.
2. Remove sand from zips, fin boxes, and moving parts.
3. Drain and dry equipment out of direct sunlight.
4. Store the board in a padded bag or safe rack.
5. Loosely hang or fold the wetsuit without sharp creases.

Never leave a board sealed in a hot car or bag. Heat can cause bubbling, delamination, and premature aging.

## Regular checks

Depending on use, inspect:

- Leash stitching and cuff Velcro.
- Fin edges, screws, and box tracks.
- Traction-pad adhesion.
- Wetsuit seams, panels, and zipper.
- Board-bag zips, padding, and ventilation.
- Spare hardware and repair supplies.

Replace safety-critical equipment rather than relying on temporary fixes indefinitely.

## Transport and storage

Use a padded bag for travel and protect fins and rails from impacts. Do not stack heavy objects on a board. Keep boards supported at multiple points, away from heat and moisture.

Keep fins, fin keys, wax, leash, and repair materials together so they are ready before a session.

## Maintenance checklist

- [ ] Equipment is rinsed after use.
- [ ] Everything is dry before storage.
- [ ] Damage is repaired or clearly marked.
- [ ] The board is protected from heat and impacts.
- [ ] Worn safety equipment is replaced.
