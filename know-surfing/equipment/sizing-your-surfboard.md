# How to Size Your Surfboard Properly

A board that floats and paddles well helps a beginner catch more waves and learn faster. Use volume and stability as starting points, then consider shape and local conditions.

## Start with the surfer

Consider:

- Body weight and height.
- Swimming and paddling fitness.
- Balance and previous board-sport experience.
- How often you will surf.
- Whether you will surf alone or take lessons.

A larger, wider board is usually more stable. A smaller board may turn more easily but is harder to paddle and balance.

## Understand the dimensions

- **Length:** Adds glide and paddling efficiency.
- **Width:** Improves stability and planing speed.
- **Thickness:** Adds flotation and stability.
- **Volume:** Measured in liters; combines overall flotation.
- **Rocker and outline:** Affect paddling, turning, and wave fit.

Volume is useful for comparison, but two boards with the same volume can feel very different.

## Choose a beginner-friendly shape

For most new surfers, a soft-top longboard, mini-mal, funboard, or stable mid-length offers a forgiving platform. Look for width through the middle, a fuller outline, and forgiving rails.

Avoid choosing a narrow performance shortboard simply because it looks advanced. Too little volume makes wave catching and balance unnecessarily frustrating.

## Consider local waves

Small, weak waves often favor length, width, and glide. Steeper, more powerful waves may suit a shorter or narrower board once your skills improve. Ask a reputable local shop or instructor to compare options for your break.

## Buying checklist

- [ ] The board floats me with room for comfortable paddling.
- [ ] The shape suits my local waves.
- [ ] The leash, fins, and boxes are compatible.
- [ ] I can carry and transport it safely.
- [ ] I have tried a lesson or rental board first.
