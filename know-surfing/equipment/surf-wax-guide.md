# Surf Wax Guide

Wax provides grip so your feet and body stay connected to the deck. Choose a formula for the water temperature, apply it to a clean surface, and replace it when it becomes smooth or dirty.

## Understand the layers

- **Base coat:** A harder layer that helps softer wax form bumps.
- **Top coat:** Temperature-specific wax for everyday traction.

Some boards use a traction pad instead of wax under the back foot. Many surfers use both, with wax under the chest and front foot.

## Choose the temperature

Packaging usually groups formulas into cold, cool, warm, or tropical water. Use water temperature rather than air temperature as the main guide. Wind and long sessions also affect comfort.

If wax becomes soft or greasy, choose a harder formula. If it stays hard and will not form bumps, choose a softer formula.

## Apply wax

1. Clean off old, smooth wax.
2. Dry the deck completely.
3. Apply a firm base coat in diagonal or circular strokes.
4. Add the temperature-appropriate top coat.
5. Build small, even bumps where you need grip.
6. Test the surface before entering the water.

Do not cover fin boxes, leash plugs, or traction-pad edges unnecessarily.

## Remove and replace wax

Use a plastic wax comb to lift old wax. Work in shade and avoid heating the board in direct sun. Wipe residue with a board-safe cleaner if needed, then rinse and dry the board before reapplying.

Replace wax when it is dirty, flattened, or no longer grips. A thick layer can hide dents and damage.

## Store wax safely

Keep wax sealed in a cool place away from direct sun and hot vehicles. Do not leave a waxed board in a hot car or tightly sealed bag for extended periods.

## Wax checklist

- [ ] The wax matches the water temperature.
- [ ] The deck is clean and dry.
- [ ] Grip is even where I need it.
- [ ] Old wax is removed when smooth.
- [ ] Wax is stored away from heat.
