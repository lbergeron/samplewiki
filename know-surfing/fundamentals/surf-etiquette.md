# Surf Etiquette and Right-of-Way Rules

Good etiquette reduces collisions and makes the lineup more welcoming. Learn local customs, stay within your ability, and prioritize safety over catching another wave.

## The basic priority rule

The surfer closest to the peak, or breaking part of the wave, generally has priority. If a wave breaks both ways, communicate clearly by calling “left” or “right” and choose only one direction.

A surfer already riding has priority over someone paddling out. Never drop in on a surfer who is already riding.

## Avoid dropping in

Before paddling for a wave:

1. Look both ways along the wave.
2. Confirm that nobody is riding or taking off.
3. If someone has priority, pull back.
4. If you make a mistake, kick out safely and apologize.

Do not paddle over the shoulder to take a wave from someone already committed.

## Paddle around the lineup

Paddle toward the whitewater or channel rather than directly through the peak. If you cannot avoid a rider, hold your board securely and paddle toward the breaking shoulder, not into the rider’s path.

Never throw your board in a crowded lineup. Keep control of it even when a wave approaches.

## Share space responsibly

- Do not catch every wave; leave opportunities for others.
- Stay in the area appropriate to your skill level.
- Give beginners and longboarders extra room.
- Follow posted beach rules and lifeguard instructions.
- Avoid crowded takeoff zones if you cannot control your board.

## Handle mistakes well

If there is a collision, check whether everyone is safe, apologize honestly, and alert a lifeguard if someone is injured. Do not argue in the water.

## Etiquette checklist

- [ ] I know who has priority before paddling for a wave.
- [ ] I can control my board when falling or paddling out.
- [ ] I leave a clear path for riders.
- [ ] I respect local rules and other beach users.
