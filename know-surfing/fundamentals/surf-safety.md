# Surf Safety Guide for Beginners

Surfing is physically demanding and conditions can change quickly. Plan every session around your swimming ability, beach hazards, and equipment limits.

## Know your limits

Be comfortable swimming in open water before surfing alone. Begin in small conditions and use a supervised lesson if you are new to the ocean.

Do not surf when sick, injured, exhausted, or affected by alcohol or drugs. Use a buddy system and tell someone where you will be.

## Check your equipment

Before every session:

- Inspect the leash, cuff, railsaver, and leash plug.
- Check the board for open dings, sharp edges, and loose fins.
- Confirm that the board suits your size and the conditions.
- Use sun protection and suitable thermal protection.
- Carry water and plan for the session length.

A leash helps keep the board nearby, but it does not make the board harmless. Keep control of it around other people.

## Fall safely

Try to fall away from the board and protect your head with your arms. In shallow water, do not dive headfirst; fall flat and check the depth before standing.

After a wipeout, surface calmly and locate your board. Hold it between yourself and incoming waves when appropriate, but do not pull on the leash blindly if it could recoil toward you.

## Manage currents and waves

Learn to identify rip currents and ask a lifeguard about local hazards. If caught in a rip, stay with your board, signal for help, and paddle parallel to shore out of the strongest flow before returning to the beach.

Never surf near swimmers, rocks, piers, or boats unless the area is designated for surfing and you understand the hazards.

## Respond to an emergency

Notify a lifeguard or call local emergency services if someone is injured or in distress. Keep the area clear and avoid creating a second casualty by entering conditions beyond your ability.

If separated from your board, signal visibly and move toward a safe exit when possible. Follow lifeguard directions immediately.

## Session checklist

- [ ] I checked weather, waves, tide, and hazards.
- [ ] I told someone my plan.
- [ ] My board and leash are serviceable.
- [ ] I have enough energy to swim back if necessary.
- [ ] I know the lifeguard location and safest exit.
