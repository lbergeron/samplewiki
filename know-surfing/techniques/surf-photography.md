# Surf Photography and Filming Basics

Good surf footage depends on timing, light, positioning, and safety. Protect your equipment and never place a shot ahead of people in the water.

## Choose equipment

For occasional use, a waterproof action camera is compact and easy to mount. A larger camera with a water housing offers more control but requires training and careful maintenance.

Useful accessories include:

- A secure wrist or body mount with a secondary tether.
- Spare batteries and memory cards.
- A floating handle when appropriate.
- Lens cloths and fresh water for rinsing equipment.
- A padded, dry storage case.

Follow the housing manufacturer’s depth, temperature, and maintenance limits.

## Shoot from shore

A telephoto lens or action camera on a stable tripod can capture useful footage without entering the impact zone. Face the light when possible, keep the horizon level, and record complete wave sets so takeoff and exit are visible.

Use a consistent location and angle when comparing progress.

## Shoot from the water

Only enter with strong swimming ability, knowledge of the break, and equipment you can control. Stay out of a surfer’s path, keep a safe distance from boards and fins, and follow local lineup etiquette.

Do not use a camera in conditions you would not surf yourself. If a housing fails, protect yourself first and abandon the shot if necessary.

## Improve your footage

- Track the surfer smoothly rather than making abrupt movements.
- Keep enough space in front of the surfer to show direction.
- Use short clips and review them after the session.
- Capture a range of angles, including setup, ride, and recovery.
- Ask permission before filming identifiable people closely or publishing footage.

## Protect equipment

Rinse housings and mounts with fresh water, especially around seals and buttons. Dry equipment before opening a housing, inspect O-rings, and store it away from heat and sand.

## Safety checklist

- [ ] My camera has a secure tether.
- [ ] I understand the break and current.
- [ ] I will not enter another surfer’s path.
- [ ] I can swim and handle the conditions without the camera.
- [ ] I have permission to publish close-up footage.
