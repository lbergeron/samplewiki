# How to Surf Different Types of Waves

Waves differ in speed, power, shape, and bottom. Adapt your board, position, timing, and expectations rather than using the same approach everywhere.

## Beach breaks

Beach breaks form over shifting sandbars. They can offer several peaks and are often accessible to beginners, but banks and currents may change quickly.

Watch a few sets before entering, identify a channel, and avoid areas where waves close out across the entire beach. Recheck your position during the session because the best peak can move.

## Point breaks

Point breaks wrap around a headland, reef, or sand point and can produce longer, more predictable rides. The takeoff zone may be concentrated, so etiquette and positioning matter.

Learn the channel, rocks, entry, and exit before paddling out. Start on the shoulder and give experienced riders enough space.

## Reef breaks

Reef breaks break over rock or coral and can be very consistent, but depth and impact hazards require local knowledge. Surf only when the tide, swell, and your ability make the spot appropriate.

Never assume a reef is deep enough. Ask a lifeguard or experienced local surfer and avoid unfamiliar shallow sections.

## Small or weak waves

Use a board with enough volume and glide. Paddle early, keep your weight centered, and generate speed with smooth trim rather than forcing sharp turns. A longer, wider board or fish shape may work better than a narrow shortboard.

## Powerful or steep waves

Powerful waves require confident paddling, earlier positioning, and controlled takeoffs. Keep your eyes on the open face, avoid late attempts beyond your ability, and know where the safest channel and exit are.

A board with more rocker or a narrower shape may help advanced surfers, but equipment cannot replace wave knowledge and fitness.

## General adaptation checklist

- [ ] I watched the break before entering.
- [ ] I understand the bottom and currents.
- [ ] My board suits the wave size and speed.
- [ ] I know my entry, exit, and safe bailout area.
- [ ] I am leaving enough room for other surfers.
