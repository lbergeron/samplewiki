# How to Improve Your Surfing: A Practical Training Plan

Progress comes from regular time in the water, useful feedback, and training that supports—not replaces—surfing. Choose manageable goals and avoid increasing intensity too quickly.

## Build a simple weekly routine

When conditions allow, combine:

- **Water time:** Practice one skill per session, such as paddling, takeoffs, or trimming.
- **Strength:** Train pulling, pushing, legs, and core with controlled movements.
- **Mobility:** Focus on shoulders, hips, ankles, and thoracic rotation.
- **Recovery:** Sleep, hydrate, and take rest days when fatigued.

If you have a medical condition or are returning from injury, seek qualified professional advice before training.

## Improve paddling

Use relaxed, alternating strokes and keep your board level. Add short intervals of stronger paddling followed by easy recovery, but stop before technique breaks down.

Practice turning the board, looking over your shoulder, and paddling into waves during normal surf sessions.

## Practice the pop-up

Rehearse a smooth pop-up on a stable surface. Record yourself occasionally or ask an instructor to check hand placement, foot position, and stance width.

Prioritize a repeatable movement over speed. A controlled pop-up leaves more attention for reading the wave.

## Improve wave awareness

Before entering, watch several sets and identify where waves peak, close out, and reform. After each ride, note what worked: position, timing, direction, and speed.

Set one specific goal per session, such as catching three waves on the correct shoulder or keeping knees bent through a turn.

## Build confidence gradually

Move to larger or more powerful conditions only after you can comfortably paddle, fall, and return to shore in your current conditions. A lesson can shorten the learning curve and identify habits that are hard to notice alone.

## Progress checklist

- [ ] I choose one clear goal per session.
- [ ] I practice technique before intensity.
- [ ] I train strength and mobility consistently.
- [ ] I rest when tired or sore.
- [ ] I progress conditions gradually.
