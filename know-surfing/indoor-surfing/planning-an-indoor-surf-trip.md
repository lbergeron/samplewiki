# Planning an Indoor Surf Trip

An indoor surf trip is easier when you plan around the venue's wave technology, schedule, equipment policy, and local conditions. Treat each park as its own surf spot: facilities differ in layout, coaching, rules, and the skills they expect.

## Research the park

Before booking, check the wave type, riding direction, session length, and ability levels. Watch recent videos when available, but use the venue's written rules for final decisions because settings and procedures can change.

Ask about:

- Beginner lessons and progression sessions.
- Board, fin, leash, helmet, and wetsuit rental.
- Minimum age, swimming ability, and health requirements.
- Locker, changing, spectator, and photography rules.
- Cancellations, maintenance closures, and weather policies.

## Book the right session

Choose a coached beginner session if you are new to artificial waves. If you already surf, ask whether the park's traveling, standing, pneumatic, mechanical, or paddle-based system matches your experience. Do not book an advanced slot solely because you are comfortable in ocean waves.

Leave time for registration, changing, the safety briefing, and equipment fitting. A shorter session with good rest can be more useful than several bookings that leave you exhausted.

## Pack and transport equipment

- Confirmation details, identification, payment method, and emergency contacts.
- Swimwear, towel, change of clothes, water, and sun protection.
- Any required medication and personal toiletries.
- Board bag and approved equipment only if the venue allows personal gear.
- Comfortable clothing and footwear for walking between lodging and the park.

If you are flying with a board, check the carrier's current size, weight, and fee rules. For a first visit, renting can avoid transport damage and compatibility problems.

## Plan the day around recovery

Indoor surfing can be demanding even when the session is short. Schedule food, hydration, rest, and a flexible activity afterward. Avoid stacking a difficult surf session with another strenuous activity if you are not used to the workload.

Keep a backup plan for a delayed session, equipment shortage, illness, or facility closure. Travel insurance and flexible bookings may be useful for expensive or international trips.

## Arrival checklist

- [ ] I confirmed the wave type and session level.
- [ ] I read the venue's equipment and safety rules.
- [ ] I know what is included in my booking.
- [ ] I allowed time for check-in and the briefing.
- [ ] I have transport, accommodation, and emergency details.
- [ ] I will follow staff instructions even if they differ from ocean practice.
