# How Indoor Surf Parks Work

Indoor surf parks use pumps, moving surfaces, air, or mechanical equipment to create repeatable waves in a pool or channel. They make practice more predictable than the ocean, but each venue has different settings, layouts, equipment, and rules.

## The main layouts

### Wave pools with moving water

Large pumps or other water-moving equipment send water around a pool or along a shaped course. The resulting waves may travel across the pool, break on a reef or wall, or form in repeated sets. Operators can often adjust wave size, speed, frequency, or direction.

### Traveling-wave and standing-wave systems

A traveling-wave system sends a continuous or repeated flow along a channel, allowing a surfer to ride along the moving water. A standing-wave system keeps water flowing over a shaped surface while the surfer stays roughly in one place. These systems can feel different from both ocean waves and one another.

## How waves are generated

- **Pneumatic systems** use air pressure or air chambers to displace water and release waves.
- **Mechanical systems** use moving paddles, flaps, wedges, or other submerged structures.
- **Paddle-based systems** push water through a pool or channel in timed movements.
- Some parks combine several methods with a shaped floor, banks, or artificial reef to control the wave.

No technology is automatically best. A system designed for beginner lessons may prioritize gentle, repeatable waves, while another may emphasize speed, barrel shape, or advanced maneuvers.

## What changes the experience

- Wave height, shape, speed, and interval.
- Pool depth, bottom contours, and riding direction.
- Session format, crowd size, and rotation rules.
- Board type, fins, and permitted equipment.
- Coaching, lifeguard coverage, and the venue's ability requirements.

Ask the park what its waves are intended for before booking. A beginner session and an advanced session may use the same facility but require very different skills.

## Before you ride

Check the venue's own rules, waiver, age and ability requirements, equipment list, and safety briefing. Follow lifeguards and instructors even if you have surfed elsewhere. Artificial waves still involve falls, collisions, moving water, and contact with a board or the pool surface.
