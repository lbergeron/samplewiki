# A Beginner's Guide to Your First Indoor Surf Session

An indoor session is a structured way to try surfing, but the water flow and equipment may feel unfamiliar. Start with a lesson or beginner slot and treat the venue's briefing as the source of truth for that facility.

## Before booking

Confirm your age, swimming ability, health and fitness requirements, and minimum experience. Ask what the session includes, how long you will be in the water, and whether an instructor is present.

Read the venue's equipment requirements. Some parks provide boards, helmets, wetsuits, and impact vests; others require particular boards or allow only approved equipment.

## What to bring

- Swimwear, a towel, and a change of clothes.
- Water and any necessary medication.
- Secure hair ties and minimal jewelry.
- A rash guard or other items the venue requests.
- Confirmation details and a little extra time for check-in.

Do not bring loose items into the riding area. Follow the park's instructions for phones, cameras, watches, and personal boards.

## Learn the basic routine

1. Attend the safety briefing and ask questions before entering.
2. Watch the flow, rider rotation, and designated entry and exit points.
3. Keep your board under control and wait for your turn.
4. Use a low stance, look where you want to go, and make small movements.
5. Fall as instructed, protect your head, and move clear of the riding line.

A coach may ask you to start kneeling, bodyboarding, or riding a larger board. Progress at the pace recommended by the instructor rather than changing equipment or wave settings independently.

## Safety essentials

Artificial waves still create impact and collision risks. Follow lifeguards, instructors, signs, and the facility's specific rules even if they differ from ocean habits. Do not enter if you are injured, exhausted, or affected by alcohol or drugs.

Keep distance from other riders, never dive into an unfamiliar area, and do not chase a loose board through moving water. If you are hurt or disoriented, signal staff and leave the water when told.

## Session checklist

- [ ] I know the swimming, age, and ability requirements.
- [ ] I know whether equipment is provided or must be rented.
- [ ] I attended the venue briefing.
- [ ] I know the fall, rotation, and exit procedures.
- [ ] I can stop and ask staff for help.
