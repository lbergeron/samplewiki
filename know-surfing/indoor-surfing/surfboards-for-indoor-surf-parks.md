# What Surfboard Should You Use in an Indoor Surf Park?

The right board depends on the artificial-wave system, your ability, and the venue's rules. A board that works well in the ocean may be unsuitable for a standing wave or a shallow channel, so confirm the permitted equipment before arriving.

## Start with the venue's equipment policy

Ask whether the session includes a board and which shapes are approved. Some parks supply soft-top boards or specialized boards matched to their flow. Others let experienced riders bring a board after an inspection or require specific dimensions, fin systems, or protective equipment.

Never assume your favorite board is allowed. Follow the park's board, leash, fin, helmet, and impact-vest instructions.

## Match the board to your ability

- **Beginner:** Choose a stable board with enough volume to float you and a forgiving deck. A soft-top can make early balance practice less intimidating where permitted.
- **Progressing surfer:** A mid-sized or slightly lower-volume board may help with turning once you can control the larger board.
- **Experienced surfer:** Use a responsive board only when the wave, equipment policy, and your skills support it.

More volume is not always better on a fast flow or standing wave. The venue's coach can explain how board width, rails, rocker, and fins affect that particular system.

## Construction and protection

Soft-top boards are common for lessons because they are more forgiving around beginners, but they are not indestructible. Fiberglass and epoxy boards may feel more responsive while requiring more careful handling.

Check the board for cracks, soft spots, loose fins, and damaged leash plugs before use. Report damage to staff and keep a board with exposed foam out of the water until it is repaired.

## Fins, leash, and safety gear

Use only the fin setup approved by the facility. Some systems use specialized fins or limit fin size because of the riding surface. Make sure screws and boxes are secure without overtightening them.

The park may require a particular leash, helmet, impact vest, or other protection. These requirements are based on the local flow and surface, not on what is standard at your home break.

## Rental or bring your own?

Renting is often simplest for a first visit because the venue can provide equipment suited to its wave. Bring your own board only after checking transport, storage, inspection, and damage policies.
